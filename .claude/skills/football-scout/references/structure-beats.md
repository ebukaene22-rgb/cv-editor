# Structure Beats — V3 Story-First (5-beat, 50s)

Every script is five beats in order. The argument is the product; the framework
is the evidence.

Write the **twitter thread first** — four tweets that form the argument.
The beats are the spoken expansion of that thread.

---

## 0. Before you write: the thread

Four tweet-style lines (≤280 chars each) that become the video's spine:

1. **Hook tweet** — the claim. A number, a name, a provocative assertion.
2. **Tension tweet** — why the popular take is wrong.
3. **Evidence tweet** — the three axis scores + key insight.
4. **Resolution tweet** — transferability + consequence + question.

If the thread isn't compelling, the video won't be. Fix the thread first.

---

## 1. claim [0–5s] — **10–20 words**

One punchy sentence that poses the case. Should stop a scroll.

**Pattern:** "[Club/player] may be paying [£X] for the wrong [position/player]."
or: "The [competition] is about to expose [player]."
or: "No scout wants to say this about [player]."

Do NOT introduce the framework here. Do NOT be neutral.

---

## 2. tension [5–15s] — **22–38 words**

Why the common belief is wrong. Set up the friction before the evidence.

**Pattern:** "Everyone [sees/talks about] X. Nobody [sees/measures] Y.
[Player] [thrives/disappears] in [context]. He [fails/struggles] when [condition]."

This is the "investigation opening" — the case file being opened.

---

## 3. evidence [15–30s] — **38–55 words**

The three axis scores enter as supporting exhibits. The framework justifies
the claim — it does not replace it.

**Pattern:** "Instinct: [n]. [Evidence sentence in one clause].
IQ: [n]. [Evidence sentence]. Gravity: [n]. [Evidence sentence]."

One line per axis. Cold and declarative. Each line should feel like a new piece
of evidence entering the case file. Do NOT editorialize — state and move on.

---

## 4. reveal [30–40s] — **22–40 words**

The transferability score drops, then the consequence is named precisely.
"Survives a move" is not enough — name the specific systems that work and fail.

**Pattern:** "Transferability: [n] percent. That [survives/does not survive] a [transfer/move].
[Drop him into X] and this works. [Ask him to do Y] and this collapses."

---

## 5. loop [40–50s] — **10–22 words**

One absolute, polarising question. No hedging. Demand a side.

**Pattern:** "Would you spend [£X] [knowing/for] [specific risk]?"
or: "[Club] or [Club] — which system does he survive?"

The question should trigger comment wars between two clear sides.

---

## Pacing note

3 words/second is the guide — but short Anglo-Saxon words punch faster than
long Latinate ones. Spoken numbers ("sixty million") are slow; use them
sparingly in the claim and reveal where the pause is intentional.
