## Part 1 — The research thesis (summary of a larger report)

**Claim:** Persistent online inefficiency does not track "entertainment markets";
it tracks *markets where the price-setter is not trying to maximise profit* (fans,
declutterers, platforms optimising engagement, hobbyists who don't know what they
have). Casual/social markets simply have the highest density of such counterparties.

**Six conditions for a persistent gap** (several must hold at once):
1. Non-fungibility (no order book can form)
2. Emotional/identity demand (buyer isn't computing return)
3. No shorting / no market maker (one-sided correction)
4. **Structural segmentation** (geography, language, KYC, regulatory perimeter) —
   claimed to be the only condition that *keeps* gaps open rather than just creating them
5. Seller's search cost exceeds their stake
6. Bounded buy-side attention

**Supporting heuristic:** when a niche has a dedicated SEO content industry
("15 Best Items to Flip in 2026"), the arbitrage has moved from doing the trade to
selling shovels. Absence of such content is the stronger buy signal.

### Plan A: Japan corridor (6 weeks to kill-or-scale verdict)

- **Phase 0 (days 1–3):** pick ONE model-number-driven vertical: watches
  (Seiko/Orient/Citizen), camera lenses, portable audio (Walkman/MD), or handheld
  consoles. Unit value $80–400. Rationale: model numbers are language-independent,
  so Japanese fluency is not required at entry; mispricing concentrates in listings
  with poor photos and no romaji keywords.
- **Phase 1 (week 1):** comp sheet for ~30 model numbers. eBay sold prices
  (Terapeak) vs Yahoo Auctions/Mercari JP asks. Landed cost = hammer + JP domestic
  ship (¥800–1500) + proxy fee + consolidation + intl ship (¥2,500–6,000) + ~5%
  UAE duty + 2–3% FX. Net = eBay sold × (1 − 13.25% − 2%) − outbound shipping.
  **Hard rule: bid only when net/landed ≥ 1.35.**
- **Phase 2 (weeks 2–5):** deploy $1,500 max across 8–12 items. Target bad-photo /
  no-English-keyword / junk (ジャンク) listings only where assessable from model
  number. Consolidate at proxy warehouse. List within 48h of arrival.
- **Phase 3 (week 6):** kill any model family under 25% net; redeploy $3k into
  winners; set alerts.
- **KPIs:** ≥60% sold within 30 days; ≥30% net margin; cycle <45 days.
  $500/mo = 7–8 items at ~$65 net.
- **Open question for you:** does selling *from the UAE* on eBay (shipping
  worldwide from Dubai) add friction the plan underweights — buyer trust, shipping
  cost/times, returns handling, eBay seller-location effects? Would routing
  differently (e.g., selling on Chrono24 for watches) be better?

### Plan B: CS2 floats (6 weeks to kill-or-scale verdict)

- **Phase 0 (days 1–2):** rails check FIRST — verify from the UAE that third-party
  cash-out (CSFloat/Skinport/Buff) actually lands money in a local bank or
  stablecoin, including KYC. If not, kill the whole lane.
- **Phase 1 (week 1):** build a float database for 3–5 skin families with steep
  float-price curves (Fades, low-float FN of liquid skins, Case Hardened patterns).
  Chart price-vs-float, mark cliff points. Own cached float data = the edge given
  inspect-link instability.
- **Phase 2 (week 2):** paper-trade for two weeks; if hypothetical margin <10% net
  after fees, repick families before deploying.
- **Phase 3 (weeks 3–6):** $2k live; positions $50–300; ≤8% in one item; ≤14-day
  holds; $5k hard inventory cap; 30% liquid buffer; circuit breaker = market index
  −15% in a month → halve inventory.
- **KPIs:** 10–15% net per flip; 1.5–2 turns/mo; $500/mo ≈ $2.5–3.5k working
  inventory.
- **Open questions for you:** (1) Is the float-gap really harvestable by a manual
  part-timer, or is it bot-dominated at the buy side (sniping underpriced low-float
  listings within seconds)? (2) Steam/Valve ToS risk of cross-marketplace trading
  at this scale — account bans? (3) Is "casual sellers price by tier, not float"
  still true on CSFloat specifically, where float is displayed by default — or does
  that mispricing only exist on venues where float is hidden?
